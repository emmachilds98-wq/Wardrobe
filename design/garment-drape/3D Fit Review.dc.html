Why the current route keeps breaking

01

Clothes are offsets from the body, not cut cloth

Every top is built outward from radial maps of his body plus a fixed ease per layer (about 0.8 cm next to skin, 1.7 cm for knits, 3 cm for jackets). There is no pattern and no drape, so each collar, hood, scarf and hem needs its own hand-written rule for how it sits. That is why shapes look "quite misshaped": a hoodie and a Harrington differ only by ease and a few detail rules.

CONTRIBUTING.md "How clothes are fitted"; 17 fix rounds logged 6–12 Oct in UPGRADE_PLAN.md; trousers rebuilt "as a pair" after weeks of faults moving around.
02

Layers are pushed apart, not collided

Each new layer is moved outside a map of what is under it. When two rules disagree about what is on top, nothing resolves them. PR #34's failures are exactly this: the collar rule ("stands on what's under it") against the hood rule ("lies over the jacket's back"). The suggested fix, lowering the jacket's collar under a hood, is a third rule for the same conflict.

occPushPt / occAddMesh; g-mod-27 (all bodies), w20261006-casual-04 (slim, heavier, athletic), hoodie-coat (heavier).
03

The shape changes after the clothes are fitted

Clothes are built on a base frame and then everything is warped to his proportions. Anything placed against another layer drifts in that warp, and every Edit Dave slider adds more drift. This is why outfits pass on Dave and fail on the slim, heavier and athletic builds.

warpMan; CONTRIBUTING.md lesson on the neck warmer carried inside a jacket collar; STATUS.md "Edit Dave's extremes".
04

Fit doesn't come from the clothes' sizes

Size charts are stored for 81 shops but only feed a line of text on the card. The model never uses a garment's chest, length or sleeve, so an L from one shop fits him the same as an L from another. "Ease-driven fit from measurements" is still on the to-do list, and the model's 34 in chest against his size L is still an open question.

data/sizecharts.json; HANDOFF.md phase 5 "Not yet"; STATUS.md "Needs Emma".
05

The checks catch glitches, not wrong shapes

The 3D check counts pokes, holes and skin pixels. A collar or hem in the wrong shape passes. The previous engineer says so directly: the passing checks missed every collar problem found by eye. Scoring the render against the shop photo was planned but not built.

tools/check_3d.js; HANDOFF.md phase 7 "Scoring against photos, approve page".
